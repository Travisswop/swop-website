Crypto link in bio: now a store AI agents can buy from

A web3 link-in-bio page has always folded a wallet into a profile. The new part is who's allowed to shop there — a human is no longer the only kind of visitor who can check out.

A crypto (or web3) link-in-bio page is a single profile link — the kind creators put in an Instagram or X bio — that has a wallet built into it instead of just routing clicks out to other sites. Visitors can tip, buy a token, or check out a product directly on the page, with the payment settling on-chain rather than through a separate payment processor the creator has to set up. The newest change to that idea is who's allowed to shop there: because the page can speak a machine-readable payment protocol, an AI agent buying on someone else's behalf can complete a purchase on it too, without a person filling out a checkout form.

What a link-in-bio page normally does

A conventional link-in-bio tool — Linktree, Beacons, and similar products — is a directory, not a storefront. It's a stack of buttons that redirect a visitor somewhere else to actually spend money: a separate store, a Substack paywall, a Cash App handle, a Shopify checkout. The link-in-bio page itself never touches the payment. It just decides where the click goes next.

That split is invisible to most visitors, but it means every sale requires a second tool, a second login, and usually a second fee on top of whatever the link-in-bio service charges to exist. The creator is stitching together a profile page and a payment stack from two different vendors.

The web3 version: a wallet where the links are

A web3 link-in-bio page collapses that stack. Instead of linking out to a payment tool, the products, tips, or drops are listed on the page itself, and a visitor buys without leaving it. Swop's version of this is called a SmartSite: one profile URL that carries a wallet, so a purchase settles directly to that wallet rather than routing through a merchant account the creator had to set up separately.

The practical difference for the person running the page is that there's no second vendor in the loop for payments. The page and the wallet are the same object.

What's new: pages an AI agent can buy from

The part that didn't exist a year ago is the buyer on the other end. Software — an AI agent doing research, comparison shopping, or executing a task on a person's behalf — can now complete a purchase from a page like this without a human present for the transaction. That's possible because of x402, an open protocol that lets a client pay for something over ordinary HTTP: a server responds to a request with the dormant "402 Payment Required" status code and a machine-readable price, the client signs a stablecoin payment authorization and retries, and the resource or product is handed over once that payment settles. No account, API key, or checkout form is involved in the payment step.

Applied to a link-in-bio page, that mechanism turns each product listing into something a machine can transact against directly, the same way it would call any other paid API. The page doesn't need a special "agent checkout" mode — it's the same purchase path a human on a browser would use, just initiated by software instead of a tap.

What changes for creators and shoppers

For a creator, the shift is passive rather than something to configure. Any product added to a page that already speaks this protocol becomes purchasable by an agent the moment it's listed — there's no separate integration to build for machine buyers versus human ones. For a shopper, human or automated, the practical change is that a purchase can complete in the same request that discovered the product, instead of a multi-step signup-then-checkout flow.

What this doesn't change is who decides to buy. An agent still needs to be instructed, within whatever limits its owner set, to spend money on a given item — the protocol handles how the payment happens, not whether it should.

FAQ

What is a "web3 link in bio" page?

It's a single profile link, the kind creators put in an Instagram or X bio, with a wallet built into the page itself instead of just a list of outbound buttons. Visitors can tip, buy a token, or check out a product directly on the page, and the payment settles on-chain rather than through a separate payment processor the creator has to set up.

How is this different from Linktree or a regular link-in-bio tool?

A regular link-in-bio page is a directory — it redirects a click to wherever the money actually gets handled, whether that's a storefront, a tip jar, or a subscription tool. A web3 link-in-bio page is the storefront: the wallet lives on the page, so a purchase completes there instead of bouncing the visitor somewhere else.

Do buyers need a wallet or account to purchase from a page like this?

A human buyer typically still needs a wallet to pay, though not necessarily an account with the seller. An AI agent buying on someone's behalf needs neither a signup form nor a stored payment method — it constructs and signs a stablecoin payment on the fly using the x402 protocol, the same request-response mechanism covered in our explainer on what x402 is.

Does Swop hold the funds a SmartSite collects?

No. Swop is fully self-custodial — keys are generated and held on your device; Swop never holds them. Payouts from a purchase go directly on-chain to the seller, not through a Swop-held account in between.
