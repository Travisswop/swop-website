El Dorado is live in beta

A virtual world built on Swop, open now in beta. Sign in with your swop.id, put on a headset if you have one, and walk around a world you entered with keys you actually hold. New improvements every week.

El Dorado is open. It is a virtual world built on Swop, it is live in beta right now at swopme.co/eldorado, and you can be inside it in about a minute.

You can walk in on your laptop, or put on a headset and walk in properly. What you need is a swop.id — the identity you already have if you use the Swop app. There is no new account to make and no password to invent.

Getting in

Three steps, and the third one is optional.

1. Open swopme.co/eldorado. It asks you to sign in with your swop.id from the Swop app. If you don’t have one yet, claim it in the app first — it takes a moment and it is the same id you use everywhere else in Swop.

2. Approve it on your phone. Open the Swop app, go to Wallet settings → Wallet connections, scan the code on screen, approve. You’re in.

3. If you have a headset, pair it. You never type a password with a controller. Sign in on your phone, then enter the short code the headset shows you — or scan from the headset directly. Then you are standing in it rather than looking at it.

Why we built it this way

Here is the part that makes El Dorado different from every virtual world you have been in before, and it is worth two minutes.

In almost every game you have played, you do not own your stuff. You hold a licence to use it. The terms usually say so in plain words. The publisher can close your account, and when that happens your inventory goes with it — not because anyone stole it, but because it was never in your hands to begin with. It lived in their database, and your account was the key to it.

Most publishers also forbid selling or moving items outside their own systems. So the way out is controlled by the same people who control the way in.

The clearest example is a city

A few years ago, some developers rebuilt Grand Theft Auto III and Vice City from the outside — reverse-engineering them so the games would run properly on modern machines. The projects shipped no game content at all. You had to own the original game for them to work.

They were taken down anyway. Take-Two filed a DMCA notice in February 2021 and GitHub pulled the code. A counter-notice brought a fork back. A lawsuit followed that September, the projects came down again in October, and the month after that Take-Two released its own remasters of both games. It was later settled.

Open the original repository today and you get “451 — Unavailable for Legal Reasons”, an error code originally meant for censorship. We checked the morning this went up.

You can take either side of that fight. The bit that matters here is simpler: every person who bought that game still only had a licence to it. The city they drove around in existed because someone else allowed it to. That is the normal condition of virtual worlds, and most people have never had a reason to notice.

What changes in El Dorado

You arrive holding your own keys.

Swop is fully self-custodial — keys are generated and held on your device, and Swop never holds them. Your way in is something you control, not an account someone else can switch off.

A few practical consequences:

No buying a token before you can do anything. Transactions on Swop are gas-sponsored. You don’t need to hold SOL or ETH to transact — Swop covers the network fee and absorbs it rather than passing it on.

It isn’t trapped in one world. The wallet you walk in with is the same one you use across Swop, which runs on Solana, Ethereum, Base and Polygon — not a balance that only means something inside one company’s game.

Losing your phone isn’t losing anything. Log in with your email on a new phone and your wallet comes back. You can also export your private key and open your assets in a completely different wallet.

What we’re not claiming

Two honest limits, because the overclaim here is easy and we’d rather you hear it from us.

Self-custody isn’t the same as legal ownership. Holding something in a wallet you control changes what can be done to your access — it can’t be quietly frozen, and it isn’t stuck inside one platform’s trading system. It does not settle what legal rights you have in a virtual item. That still depends on the terms you agreed to and the country you’re in, and the law there is genuinely unsettled. Anyone telling you a wallet turns a game item into property in the legal sense is skipping a step.

And holding your own keys means holding your own keys. Email recovery and key export exist for a reason, but the honest version of “nobody can take this from you” is “nobody can get it back for you either.”

One more, while we’re here: Swop has not commissioned a third-party security audit to date. That is true of the platform El Dorado runs on, and you should weigh it when deciding how much to bring into a beta.

It’s a beta, and it moves every week

We mean beta literally, not as a legal cushion. Things will change under you. Some things will break. Features will move or disappear.

Improvements ship every week, so the El Dorado you walk into this Monday is not the one that will be there next Monday. That is the point of opening it now rather than later — we would rather build it in front of you.

Beta describes the state of the software. It doesn’t move the consequences of rough edges onto you. If something behaves badly in there, that’s ours to fix.

FAQ

What is El Dorado?

A virtual world built on Swop, live in beta at swopme.co/eldorado. You enter it by signing in with your swop.id from the Swop app rather than by making a new game account.

How do I get in?

Open swopme.co/eldorado, sign in with your swop.id, and approve it in the Swop app (Wallet settings → Wallet connections → scan the code). On a headset, sign in on your phone and enter the code the headset shows you — no typing with a controller.

Do I need crypto experience or a seed phrase?

No. You need a swop.id, claimed in the Swop app. Swop is self-custodial, so keys are generated and held on your device, but you sign in with your identity rather than by managing a seed phrase. Transactions are gas-sponsored too, so you don’t need to hold SOL or ETH to transact.

Does this mean I legally own my virtual items?

Not automatically. In most games virtual items are granted under a limited licence rather than sold as property, and publishers can close accounts. Holding something in a wallet you control changes what can be done to your access — it can’t be quietly frozen and isn’t confined to one platform’s trading system — but legal rights in virtual goods still depend on the terms and your jurisdiction. Those are two different questions, and we’re only claiming the first.

What does “beta” mean here?

That it changes under you. Improvements ship every week, so expect rough edges and expect things to move. Beta describes the state of the software; it doesn’t make the rough edges your problem.
