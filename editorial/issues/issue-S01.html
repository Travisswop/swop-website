Usually this email opens with four prices. Today it opens with four sentences you have probably typed into a search bar at 11pm: still pending, wrong address, not showing in my wallet, swap failed. We get asked these more than anything about the market, and the honest thing to notice is that in three of the four cases nothing is broken — the money is exactly where the chain put it, and the screen simply hasn't caught up with it. So this is a service issue: four states, what each one actually means, and the one that genuinely cannot be undone. Trust is knowing which of those two you are looking at.

FACTS · Chains live
01 — “It still says pending”
Pending is not a place money can get lost in. It is a question with two answers.
A transaction is in one of exactly two states, and the explorer will tell you which in about fifteen seconds. Either it has not been included in a block yet — in which case it has not happened, and your balance is untouched — or it has been included, in which case it has happened and the thing that is behind is the screen you are staring at. There is no third state where the money is in transit, hovering, in a queue somewhere between the two. That intuition comes from bank transfers, where a payment really can sit in a clearing system for a day, and it is the single biggest source of unnecessary panic in this entire email.
One cause you can rule out immediately on Swop: you are not stuck behind a fee you underpaid. Swop covers the network fee — it is a cost Swop absorbs, not a charge passed through to you — so the classic “I set the gas too low and now it will never confirm” failure is not the explanation here. Which leaves the boring answers, and boring is what you want: a congested block, or an app that needs a refresh. Check the explorer, read which of the two states you are in, and then read the right section below.

02 — “I sent it to the wrong address”
This is the one with a real answer, and the answer depends entirely on one question: do you hold the key?
Two very different events get described with the same sentence. The first: you sent to an address you control, but on the wrong network. The funds are sitting at your address on a chain you were not looking at. Nothing is lost, because the thing that decides who can move those funds is the key — and you have it. The second: you sent to someone else's address. That is final, and no amount of escalation changes it, because there is no administrator in this system with the power to reverse a settled transfer. That is not a gap in Swop's support coverage; it is the same finality that stops anyone reversing a payment into your wallet.
Which makes the interesting work preventive, not remedial. The step that actually costs people money is transcribing a 42-character string, so the fix is to stop transcribing one: sending with a link removes the typo surface entirely. And if the chain you picked was the mistake rather than the address, the companion read is USDC vs USDC.e — two tokens, same four letters, different contracts.

03 — The other two, and three things we will not dress up
A missing token, a refused swap, and the honest limits.
🔍 “The token isn't showing in my wallet”
• Start with the chain, not the token. Swop runs on Solana, Ethereum, Base and Polygon, and a balance only appears on the chain it actually landed on — checking the wrong one of four produces a very convincing empty screen
• Then check the variant. Two tokens can share a ticker and not share a contract; the wallet is right and the ticker is lying
• Then, and only then, suspect the app. A balance that the explorer can see and the app cannot is a display bug, which is our problem and worth telling us about — a balance neither can see is on a different chain
⚖ “The swap failed — where did my money go”
• A same-chain swap is one transaction. The chain applies both legs or neither, so “the swap failed and my input is gone” is not a state a single chain can produce — if it did not complete, you still hold what you started with
• A refusal is usually a guardrail doing its job. Swop quotes Solana swaps with a 50 bps default slippage tolerance — a default, not a ceiling, and changeable. A price that moved past it gets you a declined trade instead of a bad fill
• Crossing chains is the genuinely different case, because it is not one transaction. Swop has a built-in swap — you get a quote and sign inside the app, without sending funds to an exchange or connecting to a separate site — routing Solana through Jupiter and EVM through LiFi. ⚡ Three things we will not dress up
• Swop has not commissioned a third-party security audit to date. You should know that before you decide how much to keep here, and you should expect us to say so without being asked
• A settled transfer to someone else cannot be reversed by us. We would rather name that limit than let you discover it during the worst ten minutes of your week
• Losing your phone is not losing your funds. Log in with your email on any phone and your Swop wallet comes back with it — and you can save your private key, which lets you open your assets in any wallet you choose. Recovery without a seed phrase
