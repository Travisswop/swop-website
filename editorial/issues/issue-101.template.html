Today's big story
El Dorado is open — a virtual world you walk into holding your own keys.
El Dorado went live in beta today, and the thing worth noticing is not the world — it is the door. You enter at swopme.co/eldorado by signing in with your swop.id from the Swop app and approving it on your phone. With a headset you sign in on your phone and type a short code, so you never fight a password with a controller.

In almost every game you have played, you do not own your stuff — you hold a licence to use it, and the terms usually say so outright. The clearest illustration is not an economy, it is a city. The reverse-engineered rebuilds of GTA III and Vice City shipped no game content and required you to own the original game. They were taken down anyway, and GitHub still answers that repository with “451 — Unavailable for Legal Reasons”. We checked this morning.

El Dorado starts from the other end: Swop is fully self-custodial, keys are generated and held on your device, and Swop never holds them. Two honest limits, because they belong next to the claim — self-custody is not the same as legal ownership, and Swop has not commissioned a third-party security audit to date. It is a beta, and improvements ship every week.

02 — Swop Spotlight
On a day like this, the product is the part that didn’t happen
Two posts went up on the Swop blog since the last issue, and they are the same argument told twice. Yesterday: tokenized real-world assets passed $32 billion on-chain, and the largest category of them reverts if you send it to a wallet nobody approved — custody, not the chain, decides who gets to take part. Today: El Dorado, where that dial is set the other way from the start.
The unglamorous inventory, deliberately all mechanics. Swop is fully self-custodial — keys are generated and held on your device, and Swop never holds them. Transactions are gas-sponsored: you do not need to hold SOL or ETH to transact, and Swop absorbs the network fee rather than passing it through. It runs on Solana, Ethereum, Base and Polygon. Lose the phone and you log in with your email on a new one; you can also export your private key and open your assets elsewhere. And the standing caveat: Swop has not commissioned a third-party security audit to date.
