FACTS · Platforms
01 — Why your USDC never meets a form
One of the two rails asks who you are. The other one never does, and that split is deliberate.
Crypto and x402 payments on Swop are never identity-gated — a seller is payable in USDC the moment they list a product. Only the card rail requires merchant verification. That is not a tiering of customers into trusted and suspect; it is a straight consequence of what each rail actually is. A USDC transfer settles on a public chain and nobody is extending you credit, so there is nothing for an identity check to protect. A card payment is a reversible claim on a bank, and the party carrying that reversal risk wants to know who they are carrying it for. Same storefront, two mechanics. And the storefront is already pointed at buyers who will never fill in a form at all: every SmartSite is an x402 storefront, so any product a user adds is automatically payable by AI agents in USDC over the x402 protocol, with payout going directly on-chain to the seller.
When the card rail does ask, it is worth knowing where the answer goes. Card-rail verification runs through Stripe. Stripe holds the identity documents; Swop keeps only a revocable record of the decision, not the evidence behind it. Swop never writes verification data to ENS, an NFT, or any public chain — a verification that lands on-chain is permanent and public, which is a strange fate for a driving licence. The money follows the same separation: card proceeds never touch a Swop wallet, because Stripe holds the fiat balance and pays the merchant's bank. The one thing that does not change between the two is the price. SwopPay checkout charges a 0.5% fee, and the rate is the same on the card rail and the crypto rail.

02 — Swop Spotlight
The real test isn't whether Swop works. It's whether your money is still yours on a day Swop doesn't.
Every wallet gets asked some version of “what happens if your company disappears?”, and most answers are reassurance rather than mechanics. Ours is mechanics, because it has to be: Swop is fully self-custodial — keys are generated and held on your device; Swop never holds them. An app is one way of reaching funds that are already yours, and the thing that decides who can move them is a key sitting on your hardware, not a row in our database. That is also why we can publish a gap list two sections from now without it being an apology. The gaps are about our software. They are not about who holds the money.
The practical version, for the two bad days people actually have. Lost phone: losing your phone doesn't mean losing your funds — log in with your email on any phone and your Swop wallet comes back with it. Lost patience with us: you can also save your private key, which lets you open your assets in any wallet you choose. Do the second one on a calm Tuesday rather than a frantic Saturday; a key you have already saved is worth more than any promise in this email. Our most recent Journal post makes the companion argument from the other end — that the moment a payment settles is the product, not a detail behind it: Settlement is the product.

03 — The gap list, the agent, and the four plain ones
What isn't finished yet, said by us first.
🔒 Three gaps we name before you ask
• Swop has not commissioned a third-party security audit to date. You should know that before deciding how much to keep here, and you should expect to hear it unprompted rather than in a reply to a support ticket
• ZeroProof is not live. It is in development and disabled in production, and it is not a zero-knowledge proof — today's design is a signed, short-lived, per-verifier credential, with the circuit-friendly work still ahead of us
• Tap to Pay is live on Android and is not live on iPhone. On iOS it exists in developer distribution only, so it is not a thing you can do today, and we would rather name the gap than let a feature list imply it
🤖 The agent, described without the adjectives
• It proposes; you approve. Nothing signs without a tap, and the agent never holds keys — which makes it a research assistant with a submit button, not a manager of your funds
• Each vault carries a versioned policy. Expanding a policy's authority requires the owner's signature, and every enforcement decision is written to a per-vault hash-chained audit log, so a violation is provable rather than arguable
• And the honest shape of that guarantee: the policy layer shipped Aug 28, 2026 and is running in shadow — observe-and-log — mode while it soaks; enforcement mode follows. Enforcement happens at Swop's backend signing seam, the point every agent transaction must pass to be signed. It is not an on-chain guarantee, and calling it one would be the easiest lie in this email
⚡ Four that are simply true
• Keys are generated and held on your device. Swop never holds them
• Swop covers the network fee. It is a cost Swop absorbs, not a charge passed through to you — so you do not need to hold SOL or ETH to transact
• One checkout rate, both rails. SwopPay checkout charges 0.5%, and it is the same on card and on crypto — how Swop makes money
• Four chains, one app. Swop runs on Solana, Ethereum, Base and Polygon, with a built-in swap: you get a quote and sign inside the app, without sending funds to an exchange or connecting to a separate site
