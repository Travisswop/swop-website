Backpack is the Solana exchange-and-wallet shop most people first met through Mad Lads, and this is a repricing of what it is rather than a repricing of crypto. The catalyst was regulatory, not technical: the SEC&rsquo;s Innovation Exemption opened limited on-chain trading of tokenised equities, which turned Backpack Securities from a product with a compliance question mark into a product with a lane. Sitting behind it is a stated plan to take tokenised stock listings on Solana from roughly 200 symbols to 10,000. BP touched $1.65 earlier today, an all-time high, and the price on the card above is below that &mdash; you are looking at a token that has already given back part of the move, not at the top print. Read the chips before the candle, too: a vol-to-mcap ratio that low means this happened on a thin amount of real trading, which cuts in both directions. The part worth watching has nothing to do with the price. If tokenised equities actually settle on Solana at scale, they arrive in the same wallets already holding your USDC, and the question stops being &ldquo;can I buy this&rdquo; and becomes &ldquo;who exactly am I holding a claim against&rdquo; &mdash; which is the question the Journal&rsquo;s wrapped-token post was about.
