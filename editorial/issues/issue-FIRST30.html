DAY 0 — The five minutes that decide whether this works for you
You now have a wallet that Swop cannot open. That cuts both ways, and everything below exists to make the second half matter less.
Swop is fully self-custodial — keys are generated and held on your device, and Swop never holds them. Nobody can freeze your funds, and nobody can undo your mistakes. So do one small thing now, on purpose: send yourself something tiny. Not because the amount matters, but because your first transaction should be the one where nothing is at stake, and you should make it while you are still in the mood to read a screen carefully. Swop covers the network fee, so a test send costs you nothing in gas.
Then learn the habit that prevents the expensive mistake. The step that actually costs people money is transcribing a 42-character address, so learn the version that has no address in it: send with a link. It removes the typo surface entirely, and it is what to reach for when you are sending to a person rather than a machine. Do it once now, while it is a test, and it will be what your hands do later when it is rent. One more thing worth thirty seconds: notice which of the four chains you are on. Almost every “my money disappeared” message we get is a balance sitting safely on the chain the person was not looking at. You do not need to understand four chains — you need to notice which one you picked.

DAY 2 — “It still says pending”
Pending is not a place money can get lost in. It is a question with two answers.
A transaction is in one of exactly two states, and an explorer will tell you which in about fifteen seconds. Either it has not been included in a block — in which case it has not happened, and your balance is untouched — or it has been included, in which case it has happened and the thing that is behind is the screen you are staring at. There is no third state where the money is in transit, hovering in a queue between the two. That intuition comes from bank transfers, where a payment really can sit in a clearing system for a day. It is the single biggest source of unnecessary panic in crypto, and it is wrong in a way that is permanently reassuring once you see it.
One classic explanation does not apply here: you are not stuck behind a fee you underpaid. Swop covers the network fee — a cost absorbed, not passed through — so “I set the gas too low and it will never confirm” is not what is happening. Which leaves the boring answers, and boring is what you want: a congested block, or an app that needs a refresh. And if the token simply is not there, start with the chain, not the token. A balance only appears on the chain it actually landed on, and checking the wrong one of four produces a very convincing empty screen. Then check the variant — two tokens can share a ticker and not share a contract (USDC vs USDC.e). Then, and only then, suspect the app: a balance the explorer can see and the app cannot is a display bug, which is our problem and worth telling us about.

DAYS 7, 14 AND 28 — The rest of the month
Where it lives, the second thing it does, and the half that pays you.
🔒 Day 7 — where your money actually lives
• Losing your phone is not losing your funds. Log in with your email on any phone and your Swop wallet comes back with it — and you can save your private key, which lets you open your assets in any wallet you choose. Recovery without a seed phrase
• The second half is the important half. Email recovery is convenient; a saved key is the exit. It means your assets are not hostage to Swop continuing to exist, continuing to like you, or continuing to run. Self-custody you cannot actually leave with is just custody with extra steps — save the key somewhere you would still have it if this app vanished tonight
• Two things we will not dress up. Swop has not commissioned a third-party security audit to date, and you should expect us to say so without being asked. And a settled transfer to someone else cannot be reversed by us — there is no administrator in this system with that power. Keep an amount here you would be comfortable explaining to yourself if it went wrong, and let the next three weeks tell you whether the thing works
⚖ Day 14 — the second thing it does
• If you have only sent money, you have used one part of this app. Swop has a built-in swap — you get a quote and sign inside the app, without sending funds to an exchange or connecting to a separate site. Every step where you move funds to a third party is a step where something can go wrong that you cannot see
• A refused swap is a guardrail, not a fault. Swop quotes Solana swaps with a 50 bps default slippage tolerance — a default, not a ceiling, and changeable. A price that moves past it gets you a declined trade instead of a bad fill. And a same-chain swap is one transaction: the chain applies both legs or neither, so “the swap failed and my input is gone” is not a state a single chain can produce
• Crossing chains is the genuinely different case, because it is not one transaction. Swop routes Solana swaps through Jupiter and EVM swaps through LiFi, which tells you who is actually finding your price. Worth reading once before you need it rather than during. How cross-chain swapping actually works
⚡ Day 28 — getting paid is the part that makes you stay
• Sending money is a chore; receiving it is a reason to keep an app. You can list something and be paid for it today, without applying to anyone. Every SmartSite is an x402 storefront: any product you add is automatically payable by AI agents in USDC over the x402 protocol, with payout going directly on-chain to you
• Crypto and x402 payments on Swop are never identity-gated. A seller is payable in USDC the moment they list a product. Only the card rail requires merchant verification — a deliberate split, not an oversight: the rail that touches the banking system carries the banking system's obligations, and the rail that doesn't, doesn't
• And if you take cards: verification runs through Stripe. Stripe holds the identity documents; Swop keeps only a revocable record of the decision, not the evidence behind it. Card proceeds never touch a Swop wallet — Stripe holds the fiat balance and pays the merchant's bank. Swop never writes verification data to ENS, an NFT, or any public chain.
