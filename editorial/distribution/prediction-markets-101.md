### Option 1 — r/PredictionMarkets

**Title:** How prediction market prices actually turn into probabilities

**Body:**
> Quick mental model: a "yes" share on a prediction market pays $1 if the event happens, $0 if it doesn't. Because yes + no have to sum to about $1 whenever the market is liquid, the price of yes IS the market's implied probability — 64 cents means the book is pricing roughly a 64% chance.
>
> What keeps that honest isn't consensus, it's arbitrage. If yes+no cost more or less than $1, someone can trade both sides and lock in the gap risk-free, which pushes the combined price back toward $1.
>
> The part worth internalizing before you trade: a thin order book means a modest order can move the "probability" more than the actual news would justify. Read depth, not just the headline price.
>
> Full writeup with a section on reading the order book (bids/asks/spread) plus an FAQ: https://www.swopme.co/blog/prediction-markets-101
>
> (I work on Swop, a self-custodial wallet with prediction markets built in — disclosed, but the mechanics apply to any prediction market.)

2. 1/ Yes shares pay $1 if an event happens, $0 if it doesn't. Yes + no have to sum to ~$1 whenever the market's liquid — so a yes trading at 64¢ implies a ~64% probability, straight from the price.

4. 3/ The order book under the price matters more than people think. Thin book = a modest order can move the "probability" more than the news justifies. Tight spread + depth = the price is well-supported.
