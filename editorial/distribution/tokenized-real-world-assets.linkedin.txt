Tokenized real-world assets are having a moment, and most explainers skip the only question that actually matters: a token is not the asset. It is a claim on one, and what that claim is worth varies enormously between issuers.

Start with the language. "Backed by" and "1:1" are not interchangeable. A token can be fully backed without being 1:1 — over-collateralized, with a buffer the issuer funds itself. And a token can advertise 1:1 backing while the redemption right behind it is narrow, slow, or practically unavailable to you.

Ondo's USDY is the over-collateralized case: backed mainly by short-term US Treasuries and bank demand deposits, with roughly a 3% first-loss buffer Ondo funds. That is arguably a stronger promise than a bare peg, but it is a different promise, and the distinction matters more than the marketing. OUSG is the institutional version — 24/7 mint and redeem, restricted to qualified purchasers, holding BlackRock's tokenized BUIDL fund. On equities, Ondo Stocks now lists more than 440 tokenized US stocks and ETFs across Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana, and their SEC-registered broker-dealer received FINRA authorization in July to offer tokenized equities to US investors.

Gold is where "1:1" is both true and precisely documented: each PAXG token represents one fine troy ounce of allocated LBMA Good Delivery gold, vaulted in London under Paxos Trust. It is also where the fine print deserves reading. Physical delivery starts at roughly 430 PAXG, because London Good Delivery bars weigh 370–430 ounces and Paxos cannot subdivide one. Real redemption, real minimum — both halves are true, and the honest version is more convincing than the slogan.

Collectibles run the other direction. Rather than fractionalizing an asset, the point is attaching provenance to it: the Aura Consortium — LVMH, Prada, OTB, Mercedes-Benz and Cartier — issues digital identities at manufacture so authenticity travels with the object through resale. Against provenance fraud often estimated above $500 billion a year industry-wide, a chain of custody that moves with the item beats a paper certificate.

The efficiency case is real but narrower than the pitch suggests. Roland Berger's study with Keyrock projected up to €4.6 billion, around 24%, in annual equity-trading cost savings once tokenized infrastructure scales. Worth having. Not magic. And treat headline market-size numbers carefully: published totals range from under $1 billion to tens of billions depending on methodology.
