"How does this company make money?" is a fair question, and crypto apps tend to answer it badly — usually by not answering it at all. So here is the whole thing, including the parts where the answer is "we don't."

The published number is one. SwopPay checkout charges a 0.5% fee: a seller lists something, a buyer pays for it, and Swop keeps 0.5% of that transaction. Not a subscription, not a percentage of anyone's balance, not a fee for using the wallet. And it is the same rate on both payment rails — 0.5% whether the buyer pays by card or in crypto. One number, rather than a headline rate with a quieter one behind it.

Why a transaction fee rather than a spread or float? Because the other models are not available to us by design. Swop is fully self-custodial — keys are generated and held on your device — so there is no customer balance sitting in a company-controlled account to earn anything on. And Swop covers the network fee: gas is a cost absorbed, not a charge passed through and not a margin quietly taken on top. That leaves a fee charged in the open, at the moment value changes hands.

Referrals, stated precisely rather than generously: a referrer earns half of the swap fees generated by the people they refer, for twelve months after that person signs up. That is a share of what Swop collects, not a surcharge — a referred user pays exactly what an unreferred one pays, and Swop keeps half of what it would otherwise have kept. Most referral programmes are funded by charging somebody more. This one comes out of our own share. Rewards are paid in SWOP: the fees collected are used to buy SWOP, which is credited to the referrer.

The AI agent runs on credits denominated in the SWOP utility token. That is a pricing mechanism and not an investment claim — the agent is a metered product and SWOP is the unit it is metered in, and we are not going to dress that up as anything more.

And the part most companies would leave vague: Swop charges no fee on perpetual futures or prediction markets today. Not an undisclosed rate — no rate at all. Whether that stays true is a separate question and it is something we are actively looking at, so read it as true of today rather than a permanent commitment.

That is the entire business model. One published fee, a referral share funded out of it, agent credits, and two surfaces with nothing charged yet.
