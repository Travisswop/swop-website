Three of the biggest security incidents in crypto landed within about two weeks of each other: Binance on 29 October, wallets created through Keplr's Google and Apple login (the issue was at its provider, Web3Auth), and the hijack of Across's domain on 10 November.

On what has been disclosed so far, none of them broke the cryptography. Each went through something around the keys: an engineer's laptop and the signing path behind it, a key-infrastructure vendor, a domain registrar.

That changes the question worth asking about any wallet, ours included: what can cause it to sign, who can change where you are sent, and can you leave.

We wrote up what happened, six questions to ask any wallet, what we have shipped against them, and what we have not done, including that we have not commissioned a third-party security audit.
