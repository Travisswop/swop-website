Most digital products are already being sold badly — through DMs, screenshots of payment apps, and a trust-me-I'll-send-it-after arrangement. Not because sellers are disorganised, but because the thing being sold is usually worth less than the friction of checking out for it.

Four shapes come up constantly: betting picks, your trades, the content you make, and files or templates. What they share is not a category. It is that the overhead of a conventional checkout costs more than the item.

Picks and trade calls are the clearest case for settling in seconds, because both decay. A pick is worth something before kickoff and nothing after it. An entry level is worth something before the move. Any payment rail that takes days to clear, or that can reverse weeks later, is a poor fit for a product whose entire value is that it arrived in time. It also helps that the audience for both already holds a wallet, so the step that usually kills a small sale — create an account, enter a card — is solved before you begin.

Content and files run on a different logic: not urgency, but price. Charging two dollars for one essay, one recording or one template is uneconomic on a rail with fixed per-transaction costs and a minimum that makes small sales pointless. Pricing per item rather than per month only works if the overhead of a single sale is close to zero.

Mechanically it is three pieces. Price the product in a stablecoin so neither side is guessing at a dollar value. List it somewhere a wallet can pay directly, rather than an arrangement that depends on someone remembering to follow up. And let on-chain settlement be the confirmation, instead of waiting for a card network to clear a charge.

One thing most write-ups skip: settling the payment and delivering the file are different problems. A storefront that gets the first right and leaves the second to a manual message is not finished.

And buyers increasingly are not human. Every SmartSite is an x402 storefront, so a product a seller adds is automatically payable by AI agents in USDC with payout going directly on-chain — the same purchase, with no checkout form in between.

Worth stating plainly: selling information about betting or trading is subject to the rules where you and your buyer live, and in some places promoting picks is regulated activity. The rail settles the sale. It does not license the business.
