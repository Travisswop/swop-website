Card Payments and Pricing

The price is the price. A buyer pays exactly the amount the seller entered, whether the buyer pays by card or in crypto. Tax, where it applies, is the only thing ever added on top. Swop's fee is deducted from the seller's proceeds, not added to the buyer's total. Checkout links created before September 10, 2026 kept the earlier arrangement, in which the fee was added to the buyer's total; those links show the fee to the buyer before payment.

Card payments are not yet available to every seller. Swop is introducing them gradually; the card terms below apply wherever Swop has enabled them.

One link, two ways to pay. Where card payments are enabled, a checkout link may offer both card and crypto. The buyer chooses at checkout, and the amount is the same either way. Crypto checkout pays the seller in USDC on the chain the buyer pays from, either directly or through Swop's settlement wallet as described in section 01.

Card details never reach Swop. They are entered into the payment processor's own form or payment sheet. Swop does not receive or store card numbers.

Sellers must be verified to accept cards. Identity verification is carried out by Swop's payment processor before a seller can be paid by card. This applies to the card rail only: selling for crypto on Swop does not require identity verification. Sellers who accept cards also accept the Stripe Connected Account Agreement and the Seller Agreement for Card Payments, which sets out refund, chargeback, and payout obligations.

Payouts. Card proceeds are paid to the bank account or eligible debit card the seller registers with the processor, on the schedule the processor applies to that account. New seller accounts are set up with weekly payouts and a seven-day delay, so a first payment takes longer to arrive than later ones. Card proceeds are never paid into a Swop wallet.

Limits. When enabled, card payments are initially limited to sellers based in the United States, for physical goods and services, up to $1,000 per purchase and $5,000 per seller in any rolling 24 hours. Swop may change these limits.

Refunds, Disputes, and Chargebacks

Crypto payments cannot be reversed. Swop cannot reverse a crypto payment once it is confirmed on-chain. A crypto purchase can be refunded only by a new payment: by the seller, or through Swop's marketplace dispute process from a payment Swop still holds for that order. Swop does not guarantee a refund and does not fund refunds from its own money.

Card payments can be refunded. A seller can refund a card payment through Swop, in whole or in part, back to the card that was charged. A refund cannot be sent anywhere else. Buyers should contact the seller first.

Marketplace disputes. If a buyer and seller cannot resolve an order problem, the buyer can open a dispute with Swop from the order page in their Swop account or, for a guest checkout, from the secure order link in the order confirmation email (valid 90 days; a lost link can be re-sent to the email on the order at swopme.app/guest-order). Guest disputes follow the same windows and process as disputes opened from an account. The seller can respond, Swop's resolution team decides on the evidence (automated and AI-assisted tools may review evidence and recommend an outcome, but a Swop staff member approves every decision), and each party may ask once for a human re-review. Where Swop holds a payment until delivery, it is released to the seller when the buyer confirms receipt or 30 days after the seller marks the order delivered, so open a dispute promptly. Card chargebacks follow the card network's process and override Swop's decision. This process is not arbitration and does not limit your legal rights. The full process is in section 11 of the Swop Terms of Service.

Card disputes come to Swop. A buyer who cannot resolve a card purchase with the seller may dispute the charge with their card issuer. Because Swop is the settlement merchant for card charges, the dispute is raised against Swop, and Swop bears the loss where the dispute is decided against the seller and the amount cannot be recovered from them. Sellers must provide, promptly and on request, the evidence needed to respond to a dispute, and are liable to Swop for chargebacks, refunds, and related costs on their sales. Swop may recover those amounts from a seller's pending or future proceeds.
